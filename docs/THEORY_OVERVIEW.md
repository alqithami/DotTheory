# Dot-Trace Theory Overview

Dot-Trace Theory is a formal theory of agentic social memory. Its central object is the **dot**: a persistent, socially situated trace created by an event, observation, claim, interaction, or institutional record.

The theory argues that memory-sensitive social systems cannot always be represented by agents and social edges alone. Instead, an adequate representation may require a dot field containing:

- agents,
- social edges,
- dots,
- dot-dot relations,
- agent-dot access relations,
- memory weights,
- credibility and retrievability measures,
- lineage and institutional anchoring,
- correction and repair state.

The central feedback loop is:

```text
social edges -> dot access -> retrieval -> edge pressure -> social edges
```

The theory is useful when persistent traces affect future action, coordination, trust, reputation, collective memory, institutional memory, correction, or social topology.

It is unnecessary when all transition-relevant information is already encoded in the reduced agent-edge state.
