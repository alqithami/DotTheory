# Dot-Trace Theory v0.31 Changelog

## Focus

Version 0.31 performs a literature-and-citation tightening pass. The goal was to make the related-work section more selective, accurate, and useful for the novelty claim.

## Main changes

1. **Rewrote the Related Work and Positioning section** so that each adjacent literature is tied directly to the theory's contribution.

2. **Clarified the name boundary** between Dot-Trace Theory and existing uses of ``Dot Theory,'' especially structured-representation uses of the term.

3. **Strengthened dynamic graph positioning** by distinguishing timed event/node-memory models from persistent social trace objects with provenance, access, credibility, lifecycle, mutation, correction, and topology feedback.

4. **Expanded agent-memory positioning** around generative agents, MemoryBank, MemGPT, and A-MEM, while making clear that Dot-Trace Theory adds social access, distribution, institutions, and topology feedback rather than merely another memory store.

5. **Separated graph-based memory from Dot-Trace Theory** by emphasizing the three graph layers:
   - agent-agent social graph,
   - dot-dot relation graph,
   - agent-dot access graph.

6. **Revised LLM-agent society positioning** with stronger links to LLM social simulation, AgentSociety, and boundary-aware validation for LLM-based social simulations.

7. **Strengthened collective-memory positioning** by connecting Bartlett, Halbwachs, Coman et al., Momennejad et al., and digital collective-memory work to formal dot overlap, bridge timing, access, institutional anchors, and platform curation.

8. **Improved social-capital positioning** by presenting social capital as a derived advantage generated through dot access, brokerage, reputation, bridging, correction reach, and liability.

9. **Improved institutional-memory positioning** by treating organizational records, archives, policies, logs, and boundary objects as institutional dots that may stabilize both useful and harmful traces.

10. **Added a positioning-map figure** showing how Dot-Trace Theory connects multi-agent systems, agent memory, dynamic graphs, collective memory, social capital, and institutional memory.

11. **Updated the literature-positioning table** so every row states what the adjacent literature contributes and what Dot-Trace Theory adds.

12. **Removed or replaced weak/unverified references** that were not cleanly supported by primary source checks.

13. **Corrected the Adriaansen and Smit article number/DOI** for *Collective memory and social media*.

14. **Added and/or normalized references** for Coman et al. (2016), MemoryBank, MemGPT, A-MEM, graph-augmented LLM agents, AgentSociety, and LLM-based social simulation boundary work.

15. **Confirmed successful pdfLaTeX compilation**. The compiled PDF is 225 pages.

## Result

The paper now has a more credible and professionally useful related-work section. The novelty claim is sharper: Dot-Trace Theory is not presented as replacing existing theories of memory, networks, social capital, or institutions; it is positioned as a coupling theory that treats the socially accessible memory-trace as the formal bridge object across these domains.
